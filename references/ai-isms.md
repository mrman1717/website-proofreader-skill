# AI-isms: Patterns to Detect and Remove

These are hallmarks of AI-generated text. Flag each occurrence by pattern name, then suggest or apply a natural rewrite. Rewrite to keep the meaning; don't delete content to dodge a pattern.

## 1. Overused AI vocabulary

Flag these words — almost always replaceable with something plainer:

delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), realm, navigate (figurative), unlock, unleash, leverage (verb), elevate, empower, foster, robust, seamless, seamlessly, holistic, synergy, paradigm, cutting-edge, game-changer, revolutionise (when nothing is revolutionary), transformative, pivotal, crucial (overuse), comprehensive, myriad, plethora, utilise (use "use"), facilitate, streamline, harness, embark, journey (figurative), testament, beacon, vibrant, bustling, nestled, boast(s), supercharge, dive deep / deep dive, in today's fast-paced world, ever-evolving, in the digital age.

## 2. Filler and throat-clearing

- Openers: "In today's...", "In the world of...", "When it comes to...", "It's worth noting that...", "It's important to remember..."
- Meta-commentary: "In this article we will...", "Let's explore...", "Without further ado"
- Emphasis crutches: "truly", "really", "very", "incredibly", "undoubtedly", "certainly"
- Hedge stacking: "may potentially", "could possibly help to"
- Wrap-ups: "In conclusion", "Ultimately", "At the end of the day", "The bottom line is"

## 3. Formulaic structures

- **Rule of three everywhere**: triple lists in every sentence ("fast, reliable, and secure"). Two items or one is often stronger; vary.
- **Negative parallelism**: "It's not just X — it's Y", "This isn't about A; it's about B". State Y directly.
- **Negative listing**: "No hidden fees. No contracts. No hassle." Fine once; flag when it recurs.
- **Rhetorical setup**: "So what does this mean for you?", "The result?", "Why does this matter?"
- **Dramatic fragmentation**: punchy one-word sentences for fake gravitas. "Simple. Effective. Done."
- **False agency / inanimate actors**: "the solution empowers", "the data tells a story", "this feature unlocks". Name the human doing the thing.
- **Superficial -ing analysis**: tacked-on participial clauses claiming significance ("...streamlining your workflow and boosting productivity").
- **Em dash overuse**: more than one or two per few hundred words is a tell. Em dashes (—) only — en dashes (–) are standard British English punctuation (see the proofing checklist) and are not an AI tell.
- **Quotable closers**: sentences engineered to sound like pull-quotes.

## 4. Vagueness and inflation

- Vague declaratives: "The benefits are significant", "The implications are profound" — name the benefit.
- Inflated symbolism: ordinary things described as a "testament to", "a beacon of", "at the heart of".
- Promotional adjectives with no evidence: "world-class", "industry-leading", "best-in-class", "state-of-the-art".
- Vague attribution: "experts agree", "studies show", "many believe" — with no source.
- Lazy extremes: "every", "always", "never", "everyone" doing vague work.

## 5. Rhythm and texture checks

- Three or more consecutive sentences of similar length → break one up.
- Every paragraph ending on a punchy mini-conclusion → vary.
- Each paragraph opening with a transition word (Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, However) → cut most; good ordering makes transitions implicit.
- Uniform paragraph lengths → vary.

## 6. Severity guidance

Not every flagged word is wrong in every context — "comprehensive guide" on a genuinely comprehensive guide page is fine. Judge by density: one AI-ism in a page is noise; a cluster is a pattern. In the report, group repeated instances of the same pattern rather than listing 30 identical entries.
